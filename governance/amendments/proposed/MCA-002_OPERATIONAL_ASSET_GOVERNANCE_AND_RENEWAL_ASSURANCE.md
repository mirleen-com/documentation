## 1. Amendment Notice

This document is a proposed amendment package submitted under the Amendment Process of the Mirleen Constitution. It does not amend, replace, reinterpret, or create an exception to the Constitution by its existence, publication, review, or informal acceptance.

The Constitution shall remain unchanged unless the Project Owner explicitly ratifies this amendment. Silence, discussion, repository presence, implementation work, approval of the MES name, or merger of this proposal shall not constitute constitutional ratification.

If ratified, the exact changes specified in this document shall be incorporated into `governance/MIRLEEN_CONSTITUTION.md` through a distinct, traceable constitutional commit. Any modification to the proposed text before ratification shall be reflected in a revised version of this proposal and reviewed as part of the amendment decision.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of MCA-002 is to recognize Operational Assets as first-class governed engineering artifacts within the Mirleen Engineering System and to establish enduring constitutional principles for their ownership, traceability, security, renewal, recovery, and operational continuity.

This amendment also establishes the Operational Asset Register as the authoritative evidentiary register for the current governed state of Mirleen’s Operational Assets and requires renewable Operational Assets to have documented renewal assurance and proactive reminder mechanisms.

The amendment is intentionally technology-neutral. It establishes outcomes, responsibilities, and constitutional boundaries without prescribing particular vendors, calendars, messaging systems, automation platforms, account types, or implementation technologies.

## 3. Problem and Opportunity

Engineering continuity depends upon more than source code and deployed systems. Organizations also depend upon domains, identity providers, organization accounts, cloud and DNS providers, certificates, API providers, licenses, subscriptions, payment providers, billing accounts, communication services, and other external or administrative capabilities.

These assets can fail through expiration, non-renewal, loss of access, departed personnel, obsolete billing methods, account suspension, provider failure, missing ownership, compromised credentials, contractual changes, or undocumented dependencies. A technically sound system may become inaccessible or inoperable when one of these institutional assets is unmanaged.

The current Constitution governs systems, security, documentation, ownership, change, recovery, and operational continuity in general. It does not expressly identify Operational Assets as a governed artifact class or require renewal assurance for renewable assets.

Mirleen has the opportunity to establish these protections before its asset estate becomes large, fragmented, or dependent upon individual memory. Constitutional recognition will ensure that Operational Asset Governance remains durable while allowing policies, standards, and procedures to evolve with future technologies.

## 4. Definitions

For the purposes of this amendment and the constitutional text it proposes:

### 4.1 Operational Asset

An **Operational Asset** is an institutional account, service, entitlement, registration, credential-bearing relationship, external dependency, contractual capability, or administrative resource whose availability, ownership, control, renewal, or continuity materially supports Mirleen’s identity, engineering, security, finances, communications, legal operation, or delivery of services.

Operational Assets include, but are not limited to:

- domains;
- email and communication services;
- identity providers;
- organization and administrative accounts;
- cloud and infrastructure providers;
- DNS providers;
- certificates;
- API and integration providers;
- licenses;
- subscriptions;
- payment providers;
- billing accounts; and
- future asset classes meeting the definition above.

The examples are illustrative. The definition, not the technology or vendor, determines whether an artifact is an Operational Asset.

### 4.2 Renewable Operational Asset

A **Renewable Operational Asset** is an Operational Asset whose continued validity, availability, entitlement, registration, contract, certificate, subscription, or service depends upon renewal, extension, recurring payment, periodic verification, recertification, or another time-bound continuity action.

### 4.3 Renewal Assurance

**Renewal Assurance** is documented evidence that a Renewable Operational Asset has an accountable owner, known renewal conditions, sufficient lead time, an authorized and viable renewal method, proactive reminder mechanisms, escalation, verification, and continuity or recovery planning proportionate to its importance and risk.

A reminder alone is not Renewal Assurance. Renewal Assurance addresses the complete capability to renew successfully and verify the resulting state.

### 4.4 Operational Asset Register

The **Operational Asset Register** is the authoritative evidentiary register of the current governed state of Mirleen’s Operational Assets. It records governance state and evidence; it does not create policy or redefine constitutional requirements.

## 5. Design Constraints

This amendment is governed by the following constraints:

1. The Constitution remains the supreme governing authority.
2. Engineering Philosophy may explain why Operational Asset stewardship matters but creates no mandatory requirements.
3. Every normative Operational Asset requirement shall have one authoritative source.
4. The Operational Asset Register shall be authoritative for asset governance state, not for normative policy.
5. Operational Asset records shall reference their governing requirements rather than reproduce them.
6. Traceability shall connect assets to ownership, dependencies, decisions, renewal evidence, recovery information, and affected systems.
7. Constitutional language shall remain technology-neutral.
8. Tools such as calendars, email systems, ticketing systems, dashboards, or future MES automation may implement reminder mechanisms but shall not be constitutionally mandated.
9. Governance shall remain proportional to asset criticality, impact, sensitivity, replaceability, renewal risk, and recoverability.
10. Sensitive asset information shall be protected; transparency shall not require disclosure of secrets or information whose exposure would create material risk.

## 6. Exact Constitutional Text Proposed

### 6.1 Extend the Mirleen Engineering System definition if MCA-001 is ratified

If MCA-001 is ratified before or together with MCA-002, replace the second paragraph proposed under `## The Mirleen Engineering System` by MCA-001 with the following text:

> MES comprises the Constitution, Engineering Philosophy, Governance Framework, Engineering Policies, Engineering Standards, Operating Procedures, Templates, Registers, Decision Records, Repository Baselines, Knowledge Base, and governed Operational Assets, together with the traceable relationships among them.

If MCA-001 has not been ratified, this clause shall remain pending and shall not create a standalone MES section. Ratification of MCA-002 does not by itself ratify MCA-001.

### 6.2 Extend Article VIII — Simplicity and maintainability

Append the following clause to `### Article VIII — Simplicity and maintainability`:

> 6. Institutional engineering capability shall include the Operational Assets upon which systems and organizational continuity depend. Such assets shall have clear ownership or a documented stewardship model proportionate to their importance and risk.

### 6.3 Extend Article IX — Traceability and transparency

Append the following clause after all existing and previously ratified clauses in `### Article IX — Traceability and transparency`:

> Operational Assets shall be traceable to their accountable ownership, legitimate purpose, material dependencies, lifecycle state, renewal or expiration conditions where applicable, governing decisions, continuity provisions, and authoritative evidentiary record.

This clause shall receive the next available number in Article IX at the time MCA-002 is implemented. Its meaning shall not depend upon whether MCA-001 has previously extended Article IX.

### 6.4 Add Operational Asset Governance Principles

Insert the following section immediately after `## Change Management` and its existing content and before `## Recovery & Rollback Principles`:

> ## Operational Asset Governance Principles
>
> 1. Operational Assets are first-class governed engineering artifacts within the Mirleen Engineering System and constitute institutional engineering infrastructure.
> 2. Every material Operational Asset shall have an accountable owner or documented stewardship model, a legitimate purpose, an identifiable lifecycle state, and traceability proportionate to its importance and risk.
> 3. Mirleen shall maintain an authoritative Operational Asset Register as the evidentiary source of truth for the current governed state of Operational Assets. The Register records state and evidence; it does not create or redefine normative requirements.
> 4. Operational Asset information shall be accurate, reviewable, protected according to sensitivity, and maintained sufficiently to support authorized operation, accountability, continuity, recovery, and transfer of stewardship.
> 5. Every Renewable Operational Asset shall have documented Renewal Assurance established with sufficient lead time before the relevant renewal, expiration, verification, recertification, or continuity boundary.
> 6. Renewal Assurance shall include proactive reminder mechanisms and an escalation path proportionate to the asset’s importance, renewal lead time, failure impact, and recoverability. Reliance upon unaided individual memory shall not constitute Renewal Assurance.
> 7. Renewal completion shall be verified and its resulting state recorded. Scheduling a reminder, initiating payment, or submitting a renewal request shall not by itself establish successful renewal.
> 8. Material Operational Assets shall have continuity and recovery provisions proportionate to the consequences of loss, expiration, suspension, compromise, provider failure, inaccessible ownership, or failed renewal.
> 9. Access authority, administrative control, billing responsibility, and renewal responsibility shall be assigned and traceable. Concentration of irreplaceable control in a single individual shall be identified and mitigated according to risk.
> 10. Significant acquisition, transfer, migration, renewal failure, suspension, replacement, or retirement of an Operational Asset shall be treated as an engineering change and governed according to its risk, impact, reversibility, and dependencies.
> 11. Operational Asset Governance shall remain technology-neutral. Policies, standards, and procedures may designate or change implementation mechanisms without constitutional amendment, provided constitutional outcomes remain satisfied.
> 12. Secrets, recovery codes, private keys, payment credentials, and other sensitive authentication or financial information shall not be placed in the Operational Asset Register unless an approved security design expressly provides protection appropriate to the risk. The Register shall instead identify the authorized system of custody where appropriate.

### 6.5 Extend Recovery & Rollback Principles

Append the following clause after all existing and previously ratified clauses in `## Recovery & Rollback Principles`:

> Loss, expiration, failed renewal, suspension, compromise, inaccessible ownership, or provider failure affecting a material Operational Asset shall be included in recovery planning to a degree proportionate to the asset’s importance, dependencies, replaceability, and potential impact.

This clause shall receive the next available number in the section at the time MCA-002 is implemented.

### 6.6 Recognize the Operational Asset Register in the authority model

If MCA-001 is ratified before or together with MCA-002, append the following sentence to the proposed `### Evidentiary Layer — Registers and Records` subsection:

> The Operational Asset Register is the authoritative evidentiary register for the current governed state of Operational Assets, but it is not a source of normative policy.

If MCA-001 has not been ratified, the same sentence shall be added immediately after the existing Level 5 text in `## Authority Levels`, preceded by this heading and clarification:

> ### Evidentiary Operational Asset Register
>
> The Operational Asset Register is the authoritative evidentiary register for the current governed state of Operational Assets, but it is not an authority level and is not a source of normative policy.

Ratification of MCA-002 shall not otherwise replace or renumber the Authority Levels section.

## 7. Reason for the Amendment

Operational Assets are essential to Mirleen’s engineering identity and continuity. A domain, certificate, identity tenant, billing account, organization account, or provider relationship may be as consequential to system availability as code or infrastructure. Yet these assets can remain invisible in ordinary repository documentation and depend upon personal memory, individual access, or unverified assumptions about renewal.

Constitutional recognition is appropriate because the obligation to govern this artifact class must survive changes in vendors, technologies, teams, and implementation mechanisms. The Constitution should establish ownership, traceability, renewal assurance, continuity, and evidentiary integrity while leaving field schemas, review frequencies, notification channels, escalation timing, and automation choices to lower-level governance.

## 8. Expected Organizational and Technical Effects

If ratified, MCA-002 will:

1. recognize Operational Assets as institutional engineering infrastructure within MES;
2. require accountable ownership or documented stewardship for material Operational Assets;
3. establish the Operational Asset Register as an authoritative evidentiary register;
4. require Renewable Operational Assets to have documented Renewal Assurance;
5. require proactive reminder mechanisms and escalation without prescribing a technology;
6. require verification of successful renewal rather than reliance upon scheduled activity;
7. make asset lifecycle state and material dependencies traceable;
8. bring significant asset lifecycle events under proportionate engineering change governance;
9. require continuity and recovery planning for material asset failure modes;
10. protect sensitive information by separating governance records from approved secret custody; and
11. enable future policies, standards, procedures, and MES automation to implement the constitutional outcomes.

## 9. Single Source of Truth and Traceability Model

MCA-002 preserves distinct authoritative ownership:

- the Constitution owns the enduring obligation to govern Operational Assets;
- the future Operational Asset Governance Policy will own organization-wide asset obligations and outcomes below the Constitution;
- the future Operational Asset Standard will own measurable controls and required asset metadata;
- procedures will own ordered registration, review, renewal, transfer, recovery, and retirement workflows;
- templates will own evidence structure;
- the Operational Asset Register will own current evidentiary asset state;
- Decision Records will own rationale for significant asset choices; and
- repository documentation will own repository-specific dependencies and operating context.

The intended traceability chain is:

```text
Constitutional principle
    → Operational Asset Governance Policy
    → Operational Asset Standard
    → applicable procedure
    → completed evidence
    → Operational Asset Register entry
    → related Decision Record
    → affected repository documentation
```

Not every asset requires every artifact. The depth of traceability shall remain proportional to risk and significance.

## 10. Compatibility

### 10.1 Compatibility with the current Constitution

MCA-002 strengthens existing provisions without contradicting them:

- Article I continues to establish constitutional supremacy.
- Article IV already makes material ownership, operations, security, recovery, and decisions part of documentation.
- Article V governs Recovery Points and reversibility for significant changes.
- Article VI governs least privilege, sensitive information, trust boundaries, and security.
- Article VII requires evidence appropriate to significance.
- Article VIII requires clear ownership or stewardship.
- Article IX requires durable traceability.
- Article X protects sustainable delivery and emergency governance.
- Article XI requires proportional governance.
- Change Management and Recovery & Rollback Principles already govern lifecycle change and recovery.

### 10.2 Compatibility with Engineering Philosophy

Engineering Philosophy may explain Operational Asset stewardship, institutional memory, continuity, and renewal as matters of engineering trust. It shall not create renewal deadlines, mandatory fields, approval rules, or other normative requirements.

### 10.3 Compatibility with MCA-001

MCA-002 does not assume MCA-001 has constitutional authority. Conditional integration text is expressly identified. If both amendments are ratified, MCA-002 integrates Operational Assets into the MES definition, Single Source of Truth model, traceability model, and evidentiary layer without changing MCA-001’s purpose or boundaries.

If MCA-002 is ratified first, its independent constitutional changes can be implemented without incorporating unratified MCA-001 text.

### 10.4 Compatibility with the authority hierarchy

MCA-002 adds no new normative authority level. The Operational Asset Register is evidentiary. Policies and standards remain subordinate to the Constitution, and implementation technologies remain subordinate to policies, standards, and procedures.

No constitutional exception is requested or created by this amendment.

## 11. Technology Neutrality

The Constitution shall not prescribe how renewal reminders, escalation, evidence capture, credential custody, or asset monitoring are technically implemented.

Implementation may use calendars, email, messaging, ticketing, dashboards, identity platforms, financial systems, repository automation, dedicated asset-management systems, or future MES automation. Selection and configuration of such mechanisms belong in policies, standards, procedures, architectural decisions, and repository-specific documentation.

A change in implementation technology shall not require constitutional amendment when the constitutional outcomes remain satisfied.

## 13. Risks and Mitigations

### Risk 1 — The Register may be mistaken for policy

**Mitigation:** The amendment expressly classifies the Register as evidentiary and prohibits it from creating normative requirements.

### Risk 2 — Sensitive information may be exposed

**Mitigation:** The Constitution will prohibit unprotected placement of secrets and sensitive credentials in the Register and permit references to approved custody systems.

### Risk 3 — Asset inventory may become excessively broad

**Mitigation:** Materiality and proportionality govern classification depth and required evidence.

### Risk 4 — Reminder mechanisms may create false assurance

**Mitigation:** The amendment distinguishes reminders from Renewal Assurance and requires verified renewal outcomes.

### Risk 5 — A single reminder mechanism may fail

**Mitigation:** Policies and standards may require redundancy and escalation proportionate to criticality without hard-coding technologies in the Constitution.

### Risk 6 — Ownership may be nominal rather than effective

**Mitigation:** Renewal Assurance and traceability require viable authority, access, renewal method, escalation, and verification, not merely an owner’s name.

### Risk 7 — Provider dependency may be underestimated

**Mitigation:** Material dependencies, replaceability, continuity, recovery, and provider-failure scenarios are constitutionally recognized.

### Risk 8 — MCA-001 and MCA-002 implementation may conflict

**Mitigation:** Conditional integration instructions separate independent MCA-002 changes from MCA-001-dependent text. If both are ratified, they shall be implemented in amendment order with a verified combined diff.

7. Establish the Operational Asset Standard only after its governing policy exists.

10. Inventory material Operational Assets proportionately and assign accountable ownership or stewardship.
11. Establish Renewal Assurance for existing Renewable Operational Assets according to criticality and nearest renewal boundary.
12. Update affected repository documentation to reference registered asset dependencies without duplicating authoritative register state.
13. Review the system after initial adoption for missing assets, inaccessible ownership, renewal exposure, secret-handling risk, and continuity gaps.

## 15. Recovery Point

The Recovery Point for implementation of MCA-002 shall be the exact Git commit on the default branch immediately preceding its constitutional implementation commit.

Before the Constitution is modified, the implementation record shall capture:

1. the pre-amendment constitutional blob SHA;
2. the pre-amendment repository commit SHA;
3. the ratified MCA-002 proposal version and commit SHA;
4. the ratification state and implementation commit of MCA-001;
5. the exact intended constitutional diff;
6. the verification plan for insertion points, numbering, and cross-references; and
7. the person authorized to initiate recovery.

Recovery shall be initiated if implementation differs from ratified text, incorporates unratified MCA-001 text, breaks constitutional structure or references, exposes sensitive information, or unintentionally weakens an existing protection.

Recovery restores the pre-amendment Constitution and affected files while preserving the proposal, ratification, implementation, and recovery history. Recovery of implementation does not revoke the Project Owner’s ratification unless the decision is explicitly withdrawn or superseded.
